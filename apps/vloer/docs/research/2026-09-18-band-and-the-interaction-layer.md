# BAND and the agent interaction layer: what it claims, what it ships, and what Glide should take

> Survey date: **18 September 2026**. Method: nine research agents in parallel (BAND's own documentation, its source repositories and SDKs, its competitors, the interoperability standards landscape, the coding-agent workbench category, and a read-only sweep of this repository), plus first-hand verification of BAND's published OpenAPI document and the Agent Host Protocol implementations page. The condensed verdict lives in the [market landscape shortlist](market-landscape.md#4-shortlist-products-that-should-influence-the-decision) and the [protocol appendix](2026-09-11-ecosystem-alternatives.md#appendix-adjacent-components-and-communication-protocols).
>
> Landing contract: this repository's `AGENTS.md` carries no protocol-research contract block. The destinations below were inferred from existing practice and are stated here so the next sweep does not have to guess: dossier in `apps/vloer/docs/research/`, product ledger in [market-landscape.md](market-landscape.md), protocol ledger in [2026-09-11-ecosystem-alternatives.md](2026-09-11-ecosystem-alternatives.md), watchlist as a numbered item in [Ploeg's backlog](../../../ploeg/docs/backlog.md).

## Verdict

**Reject BAND as a dependency and mine it for design.** It fails on fit before it fails on maturity: BAND sells an agent-to-agent interaction layer, and neither Vloer nor Ploeg has an agent-to-agent seam to sell it into. It then fails on maturity as well, and by an unusually wide margin. This is the [Paperclip outcome](../../../ploeg/docs/adrs/0009-paperclip-mine-for-design-never-integrate.md) rather than the [A2A outcome](../../../ploeg/docs/adrs/0007-a2a-adopt-nothing-watchlist-a-facade.md): there is no honest facade to watchlist, because BAND is a product with its own control plane rather than a protocol anyone else implements.

The separate and more useful finding is what the sweep established about the layer itself. **Authority, budget, provenance and ticket-to-merge delivery are claimed by no protocol and no standards body in this landscape, and no working group is chartered to claim them.** Those four are exactly what Ploeg implements. That is not a gap waiting to be filled by a standard; on current evidence it is stable ground.

## 1. What BAND actually is

Thenvoi AI Ltd., trading as BAND. $17M seed announced April 2026 (Sierra Ventures, Hetz Ventures, Team8), founded mid-2025 by Arick Goomanovsky (previously Sygnia, Ermetic) and Vlad Luzin. Tel Aviv and San Francisco. Self-description, verbatim from [the home page](https://www.band.ai/): *"Enterprise-grade interaction infrastructure that enables real-time, multi-peer collaboration between agents and humans through a shared interaction layer with built-in governance."*

Two layers in their own terms: an **Agentic Mesh** for collaboration and an **Interaction Control Plane** for governance. Five primitives, spelled as [their core concepts page](https://docs.band.ai/core-concepts) spells them: Agent (a definition of prompt, model and tools), Execution (an isolated runtime instance scoped to one room), Chat Room, @mention routing, and Contact (a bilateral consented connection). Transport is REST for agent-to-platform commands and Phoenix Channels over WebSocket for platform-to-agent events, against an Elixir backend.

### The published contract

A real OpenAPI 3.1.0 document is served at [docs.band.ai/openapi.json](https://docs.band.ai/openapi.json) — 243,905 bytes, 56 paths, 251 schemas, server `https://api.band.ai`. It is not in any repository, has no git history, no tags and no changelog, so it cannot be pinned or diffed. Schema names are machine-mangled from the closed backend. The WebSocket half has no machine-readable schema at all, only [prose](https://docs.band.ai/websocket/overview), and event payload normalisation lives in a **closed Rust crate** (`band-sdk-core`, its repository returns 404) which the SDK documentation names as the arbiter while calling its own models "rule-free typed projections."

### What the API surface actually contains

Counted directly against the OpenAPI document rather than taken from marketing:

| Vocabulary | Occurrences in BAND's entire published API |
| --- | --- |
| `spend`, `usd`, `billing`, `token_usage` | **0** |
| `budget` | 2, both the per-message *exchange counter* on the delegation endpoint — an anti-abuse cap on OAuth grants, not money |
| `attestation`, `provenance`, `in-toto`, `slsa`, `sbom` | **0** |
| `repository`, `commit`, `pull_request`, `merge`, `worktree` | **0** |
| `webhook`, `tracker`, `exactly-once` | **0** |
| `approval`, `policy`, `retention` | **0** |
| `delegation` | 61 |

The 56 paths are chats, messages, participants, contacts, memories, peers, a task board, one delegation-token exchange, and a profile. The "audit trail" resolves to an append-only goal-audit on a room board and soft-deleted memories retained for history. It is a chat server with a Kanban board and an OAuth-for-agents exchange.

The delegation exchange is the strongest thing in the product and should not be dismissed: [`POST /api/v1/agent/delegation/exchange`](https://docs.band.ai/api/agent-api/agent-api-delegation/exchange-delegated-token) lets a delegated agent trade a message-scoped grant for a provider token to act on the originator's behalf, bounded by a time window, a per-agent rate limit and a per-message exchange cap, and requires that the calling agent was @mentioned on the message and that the message crossed an ownership boundary.

### What BAND says it does not do

From [their governance page](https://www.band.ai/use-cases/ai-governance-standardization), verbatim: *"Keep policy enforcement in your application logic, and use BAND to make the handoff record visible."* And from [their enterprise page](https://www.band.ai/solutions/enterprise-platforms): *"Use BAND for the shared collaboration record, not as a replacement for every enterprise control system. Your existing systems can still own policy, identity source of truth, and approvals."* Asked whether BAND makes approvals easier to audit, their own FAQ answers *"Yes, **if** the approval is posted in the room"* — a condition, not a guarantee. Their sharpest line about their own scope is the honest one: *"This does not make agent decisions correct. It makes them attributable, which you cannot add later."*

**The split between what the platform enforces and what is marketing is worth stating precisely, because it is not the split the marketing implies.**

*Genuinely enforced, server-side and fail-closed:* mention-scoped visibility; a human/agent authentication split where agent keys are rejected with 403 on every `/me` endpoint; the bilateral Contacts consent state machine; mention validation as the actual loop-prevention mechanism; existence-hiding on the delegation endpoint, where "unknown message", "not delivered to you" and "you were not mentioned" are deliberately indistinguishable. And above all the **delegated token exchange**, which verifies eight conditions before returning the originator's provider token, including that the exchange falls inside a processing window measured from a server-minted `delegation.minted_at` that is *"stripped from every client write"*. That is real capability-bound mechanics, and it is also orphaned: no SDK binding, no tool, no conceptual documentation page and no changelog entry.

*Marketing only:* **"Interaction Control Plane" appears zero times in the 186-page documentation corpus** — it exists solely as a layer name on the platform page. **"RBAC" likewise appears zero times in the docs.** "Capability-Bound Execution" has one sentence and no mechanics, and another BAND page reframes capabilities as a routing hint rather than a boundary: *"Capabilities tell BAND what each agent can do, so requests route to the right agent."* The "delegation chain" has no data model, no propagation format and no depth limit. Approval gates are explicitly convention — the published contract is hand-written YAML room metadata with no schema and no endpoint, and the specialist waiting for approval is prompt discipline. **There is no audit-log API at all**: no export format, no retention guarantee, no tamper-evidence, no immutability claim beyond an append-only task history.

*And the compliance posture, for a product sold as enterprise governance:* **zero SOC 2, ISO 27001, HIPAA, GDPR or FedRAMP claims anywhere on the site or in the documentation, and no trust centre** — the footer carries only a privacy policy, terms of service and a security-issue link. The Python SDK is still published with a `Development Status :: 3 - Alpha` classifier, and [BAND's own documentation](https://docs.band.ai/integrations/sdks/tutorials/strands) says *"the `band-sdk` package is still published with an alpha development-status classifier, so pin a version in production"* while every install line in those same docs is unpinned.

### Deployment

SaaS only. Pricing is $0 free (single user, explicitly non-commercial), $17.99/month Pro (explicitly personal), and a custom Enterprise tier offering custom retention rather than custom hosting. There is no self-host documentation, no deployment guide, no container image, no chart, no licence and no tier for running the platform, and the server is closed-source Elixir. The single aside is an environment-variable note that `BAND_REST_URL`/`BAND_WS_URL` should change *"only when connecting to a different environment, such as a self-hosted deployment"*, alongside a capability flag (`ff_file_transfer`) documented as *"an on-prem-only deployment flag, off everywhere on SaaS today."* On-prem exists as an enterprise arrangement; no artifact is published. Every message between your agents, including the delegated-authority record sold as your audit trail, transits `app.band.ai`.

### Governance and adoption, measured

- **Single vendor.** 5 of 718 commits on the Python SDK come from outside `@band.ai` addresses, about 0.7%, and one of those authors is named as a decision-maker in an internal architecture document. The TypeScript SDK has zero non-staff human committers. No `GOVERNANCE.md`, no `CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md`, no `CODEOWNERS`, no `SECURITY.md`, no RFC process, no public roadmap. Work is tracked against a private `INT-`/`JAM-` ticket system.
- **Zero external dependents**, confirmed independently against the GitHub dependents graph for both SDKs and against ecosyste.ms (`dependent_packages_count: 0`, `dependent_repos_count: 0`).
- **Downloads are their own CI.** PyPI `band-sdk` peaked at 4,954 in June 2026 and is running at roughly 860/month in September, about 83% off the peak. On npm, six of the last 30 days had exactly zero downloads and the weekly total is carried by two spikes landing on publish dates. The transitive children outdraw the parent five to one, which organic installs cannot do.
- **Every "supported framework" is first-party.** LangGraph, CrewAI, Pydantic AI, Anthropic, n8n and the rest are extras *inside* `band-sdk` that BAND wrote. BAND imports CrewAI; CrewAI does not ship BAND. Third-party-authored integrations found: zero.
- **Stars:** `band-sdk-python` 12, `band-sdk-typescript` 1, flagship `codeband` 65.

### Jam, and the retreat that matters

[Codeband](https://github.com/band-ai/codeband) is BAND's own ticket-to-merged-PR product built on the mesh: adversarial cross-model review (Claude Code writes, Codex reviews and vice versa), a planner with an opposite-family plan reviewer, a git worktree per coder, PRs opened per agent, and risk-tiered auto-merge where *"low-risk PRs can auto-merge; medium, high, and critical changes wait for approval."* It is their most-starred repository by five times. Its README now reads: ***"Status: maintenance only. Codeband is not actively developed... If you are starting something new, use Jam instead."*** The stated reason is that Jam *"covers the part of Codeband that mattered most — getting agents to work together — without asking you to adopt Codeband's orchestrator, config, or worktree layout."*

[Jam](https://www.band.ai/use-cases/band-desktop) does not deliver code. It is a Tauri desktop shell over a Rust daemon (`jamd`) with a `jam` CLI and a `band-peer` Claude Code plugin, distributed as **proprietary prebuilt binaries** (`license "LicenseRef-Proprietary"` in the Homebrew formula, not notarised as of v0.4.8, source in a private repository). It offers a kanban board, a visual map of which component each agent session owns, and human intervention points. Its sandboxing is Docker Sandboxes via the `sbx` CLI with a genuinely well-considered credential-custody design: the guest only ever sees the literal string `proxy-managed` and an egress proxy substitutes the real key, with deny-by-default egress allowlists. It statically links a private fork of [ccusage](https://github.com/thenvoi/ccusage) for local token-cost analysis, which is cost *visibility* and not budget authority.

The sequence is the finding. The coordination vendor built the delivery loop, then put it in maintenance mode and redirected users to a product that watches coding agents instead of finishing their work.

## 2. Layer map

Compiled from reading the current text of MCP `2026-07-28`, A2A v1.0.1, ACP schema v1.22.0/v2.0.0-alpha.4 and AHP v0.9.0, against this repository's implementation.

| Layer | Claimed by | Glide's position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Model and inference | Nobody; proprietary APIs | LiteLLM as gateway ([Ploeg ADR-0008](../../../ploeg/docs/adrs/0008-litellm-is-the-credential-and-metering-seam.md)) |
| Agent to tools and context | **MCP 2026-07-28** (Linux Foundation / AAIF) | Below the harness; [PV-063](../../backlog/README.md) proposes read-only MCP tools |
| Harness and agent runtime internals | Unclaimed by design | `AgentRuntime` (Vloer), `harness.Adapter` (Ploeg) |
| Sandbox and workspace | **Unclaimed, and retreating** — ACP v2 removed the client filesystem and terminal surface | [ADR-0009](../adrs/0009-workspace-placement-is-a-session-choice.md), [ADR-0013](../adrs/0013-sandbox-crd-placement-with-warm-kata-pools.md) |
| Agent to editor/harness client | **ACP** (Zed + JetBrains), ~47 registered agents | Ploeg's `pkg/harness/adapters/acp` |
| Multi-client session coordination | **AHP 0.9.0** (Microsoft, single vendor) | Vloer's host, [ADR-0012](../adrs/0012-agent-host-protocol-host.md) |
| Agent to agent peer | **A2A 1.0.1**, thinly deployed; MCP Agents WG circling; BAND sells a proprietary version | **Nobody in this estate.** An explicit AHP anti-goal, and explicitly rejected for factory internals by [Ploeg ADR-0007](../../../ploeg/docs/adrs/0007-a2a-adopt-nothing-watchlist-a-facade.md) |
| Human interaction surface | AG-UI 1.0.0 (CopilotKit, no foundation); AHP and ACP for editors | Vloer's browser and VS Code surfaces |
| Agent identity and discovery | A2A Agent Card, MCP Server Card WG, AI Catalog, Web Bot Auth | Not applicable; one trust boundary |
| Authorization to call a tool or agent | MCP (OAuth 2.1, RFC 9728, CIMD), AHP (RFC 9728, RFC 6750) | Bearer tokens; [ADR-0016](../adrs/0016-sign-in-and-link-your-own-accounts.md) for people |
| **Authority, policy, leases, claims** | **NOBODY** | Ploeg ADR-0010, ADR-0024 |
| **Provenance and attestation of artifacts** | **NOBODY** | [Vloer ADR-0014](../adrs/0014-signed-candidates.md), Ploeg ADR-0027 |
| **Ticket to branch to PR to merge** | **NOBODY** | Ploeg's delivery contract |
| **Cost and model-spend attribution** | No protocol. Three *gateways* enforce caps; six report only | Ploeg ADR-0012, Vloer's broker over LiteLLM |
| **Task-bound budget: a cap minted and revoked with the ticket** | **NOBODY.** ServiceNow attributes spend per agent, user and department; every budget anywhere is per-user, per-org or per-agent on a calendar cycle | Ploeg's Inference Account |

The four bold rows are the finding. Details, verified by grepping the full spec corpora:

- **Cost, in agent protocols: zero.** ACP carries `UsageUpdate { used, size, cost? }` with an ISO-4217 amount, advisory and per-session. AHP carries `UsageInfo { inputTokens, outputTokens, model, cacheReadTokens }` — tokens and a model name, no money. A2A: zero hits for `cost`, `budget`, `billing`, `price` across the full spec. MCP: nothing, and none of its 11 working groups or 7 interest groups is chartered for it. The two reporting fields are not even union-compatible: ACP gives money without a model, AHP gives a model without money.
- **Cost, in payment credentials: a real precedent, and it is worth reading before inventing the shape.** [AP2 v0.2.0](https://ap2-protocol.org/) (tagged 2026-04-28, donated to the FIDO Alliance the same day) restructured its mandate model into Open and Closed mandates as SD-JWT VCs. An Open Mandate expresses four of the five properties Ploeg's budget seam needs in one user-signed credential: **scoped** (`allowed_merchants`, `line_items`), **time-bounded** (`iat`/`exp`, with spec guidance to set `exp` to the smallest workable value), **budget-capped** (`payment.budget{max,currency}`, `amount_range{min,max}` in minor units, `agent_recurrence{frequency,max_occurrences}`), and **bound to a specific agent** (a `cnf` claim carrying `agent_pk`, sender-constraining the mandate; the agent later mints a Closed mandate signed with `agent_sk` and bound to the open one by `sd_hash`). The fifth is missing: **there is no revocation and no mandate status endpoint anywhere in the specification — expiry is the only kill switch.** The domain is merchant payments, so this is not adoptable, but the open/closed pair is the only published design precedent for [ADR-0012's authorized-then-settled split](../../../ploeg/docs/adrs/0012-two-level-budgets-authorized-and-settled.md) and [ADR-0013's per-run minted push rights](../../../ploeg/docs/adrs/0013-push-rights-are-minted-per-run.md). Read it, then add the revocation endpoint it lacks.
- **Cost enforcement does exist — in gateways, which is exactly where we put it.** Four products enforce real caps, and every one of them is a gateway: **Maxim/Bifrost** (Apache-2.0, per-team, per-project and per-model limits plus virtual keys carrying independent budgets), **agentgateway** (Apache-2.0, Linux Foundation, explicit budget and spend controls), **Kong AI Gateway 2.0** (GA 1 September 2026, cost attribution keyed to authenticated principals, with modality-aware pricing), and **LangSmith LLM Gateway** (public beta, spend and rate limits at organisation, workspace, API-key and user level, with per-trace cost attribution computed from token counts against configured model pricing). n8n shipped **Gateway Credits** on 17 September 2026 with per-workflow spend visibility at provider list price, though it tops up rather than stopping. Six more report without enforcing: Langfuse (budget *alerts*, not caps), Arize AX's Cost Agent, AgentOps, Obot, Cloudflare AI Gateway, HoneyHive. Nothing is documented for AWS Bedrock AgentCore — thirteen separately-billed meters and **no agent dimension** — nor for Microsoft Foundry, LangSmith, or **any** identity vendor surveyed. This vindicates [Ploeg ADR-0008](../../../ploeg/docs/adrs/0008-litellm-is-the-credential-and-metering-seam.md): the gateway is the right seam, and LiteLLM's virtual keys are the same primitive Maxim and Kong charge for.
- **The join is narrower than unclaimed, and the precise gap matters.** An earlier draft of this dossier claimed nothing anywhere joins spend to a delegated identity. That is wrong, and the correction is worth carrying: **ServiceNow AI Control Tower attributes token spend — first-party and third-party — by asset, user and department**, which is the best in this survey. Salesforce and Atlassian do a partial version through credit consumption per agent. So per-agent and per-user attribution exists in the enterprise platforms; it is the gateways and the identity vendors that do not join them. What **no** product does, confirmed separately against the coding-workbench category, is bind a budget to a **task**, mint a scoped credential for that task, and revoke it when the task closes. Every budget found anywhere is per-user, per-organisation or per-agent, on a monthly or lifetime cycle. That narrower claim — **task-bound budget with a credential lifecycle, attributed back to the originating ticket** — is Ploeg's Inference Account, and it is still unoccupied. State it that way rather than the broader version, which a ServiceNow customer would immediately contradict.
- **The same shape outside payments, as individual IETF drafts.** None is working-group adopted, so treat these as signal rather than standard. `draft-kroehl-agentic-trust-aae-02` (Agent Authorization Envelope) carries a MANDATE block plus CONSTRAINTS (`max_transaction_value`, `allowed_domains`, `rate_limit`) and VALIDITY with `revocation_check` and `single_use` — all five properties. `draft-nelson-agent-delegation-receipts-10`, at revision 10 the most-iterated of the set, is **essentially Ploeg's evidence-and-receipt model written as an Internet-Draft**: a user-signed Authorization Object created before any action, anchored to a tamper-evident append-only log, deny-by-default allowlists, a time window validated against an RFC 3161 timestamp authority rather than client clocks, a mandatory revocation check as the *first* verification step, and a strictly decreasing capacity gate. Note also that `draft-oauth-ai-agents-on-behalf-of-user` is **expired** despite being widely cited in 2026 writing.
- **Provenance.** Zero across all four spec corpora for `provenance`, `attest*`, `in-toto`, `SLSA`, `sigstore`. AHP uses the word "provenance" twice, both times meaning which response part produced a piece of text. The only cryptographic objects in the landscape are A2A's optional Agent Card JWS and the AI Catalog Trust Manifest, and both attest *who the agent is*, never *what the agent produced*.
- **Leases and claims.** MCP Tasks gives a durable handle with a TTL and cooperative cancellation, and has **zero clients on MCP's own extension support matrix**. A2A offers `MAY`-level idempotency, meaning a retried send can legitimately double-execute. AHP's new automation channel has run claims that are explicitly host-internal and never on the wire. No protocol has a lease TTL, a fencing token, mutual exclusion or crash-recovery ownership.
- **Delivery.** AHP's changeset channel looks closest and is not close: `ChangesetOperation` is a server-defined opaque entry with an id, a label and a status, and the spec's own comment offers "View Pull Request" as an illustration. The protocol carries a list of buttons the host invented. Its VCS awareness is a `kind` enum knowing `branch` and `uncommitted`.

## 3. Component-by-component fit

**Vloer's session engine — competing in intent, incompatible in shape.** Crew roles execute strictly sequentially: [`engine.ts:81`](../../src/engine.ts) rejects any crew whose roles after the first are not read-only, [`engine.ts:733`](../../src/engine.ts) is a single `await`ed `for` loop with no `Promise.all` over roles anywhere, and one workspace is prepared above the loop against a single branch (`vloer/${id}`). Two writers on one working directory is not expressible. BAND's model is many agents in one room addressing each other by @mention. Adopting it would not extend the engine; it would replace the thing the engine is.

**Ploeg's authority — orthogonal, and BAND concedes the seam.** Ploeg holds Work Items, Shifts, Runs, Leases and outcomes in PostgreSQL, with a unique key on `leases` that makes a second writer physically impossible, and `plan.Validate` refusing a round that mixes a writer with readers. BAND has no queue, no lease, no claim and no exactly-once semantics, and its own page says policy enforcement belongs in your application. There is nothing to delegate to it.

**The blackboard, head to head.** [Ploeg ADR-0011](../../../ploeg/docs/adrs/0011-the-pull-request-is-the-blackboard.md) settles that coordination happens through the pull request and the database: a reading Run returns `OutcomeReport.Findings`, Ploeg posts them as a PR comment and injects them into the next round's `TaskSpec.Briefing`, and *"the agent never calls a forge or a Ploeg API to fetch them."* The seam map confirms the consequence: **no agent-to-agent channel exists in either application, and no agent can address another agent.** BAND solves the same problem with a chat room. Same problem, two substrates, and ours is queryable, schema-checked and signable while theirs is a transcript.

**The AHP host — adjacent, and they do not know each other exists.** BAND's corpus of 186 documentation pages, 15 marketing pages and seven repositories contains **zero mentions of "Agent Host Protocol" or "AHP"**, and Microsoft's implementations page lists no agent-mesh platform. AHP's own documentation disclaims BAND's seam: *"AHP is a coordination layer. ACP is a communication layer. They compose naturally."*

**The LiteLLM broker — no counterpart.** BAND's documentation mentions LiteLLM three times, all the same line in an onboarding prompt noting that CrewAI model strings take a litellm prefix, plus transitively in the vendored ccusage fork. Zero mentions in the documentation corpus and zero on the site. BAND has no gateway story, no BYO-routing, and pushes the cost question away: its Codex adapter page says *"Billing and model access follow the sign-in method you selected during `codex login`."*

**Candidates and attestations — no counterpart, and the contrast is sharp.** Zero mentions of in-toto or SLSA anywhere in BAND's corpus, which is notable for a product whose pitch is a chain of custody for delegated authority.

**Task connectors — they built a board instead.** Zero mentions of Forgejo and zero of Vikunja, despite BAND shipping its own chat-task and board API. They integrate no tracker.

**OpenCode — the one real point of contact.** BAND ships a first-class `OpencodeAdapter` with a [dedicated tutorial](https://docs.band.ai/integrations/sdks/tutorials/opencode) linking opencode.ai, an install extra, and integration tests including an approval round-trip. They integrate the harness we run, connecting to a local OpenCode server over HTTP.

**Dependency constraints — a hard stop.** Vloer's [package.json](../../package.json) has no `dependencies` key at all, [ADR-0002](../adrs/0002-native-node-and-single-writer-storage.md) records the choice, and [`scripts/check.mjs:44`](../../scripts/check.mjs) fails CI with *"production npm dependencies require an architecture decision"* on any entry. `@band-ai/sdk` is 0.4.1 after six months, has no LICENSE file at its repository root, carries seven transitive runtime packages, and two of them are compiled artifacts from a private repository. Ploeg has no Go SDK available at all, and BAND's own architecture document contains an appendix titled *"What a Go SDK would change"* opening ***"Not on the roadmap."***

## 4. The one honest scenario

If Glide ever needs an agent it does not run to participate in a crew — a client's own specialist, or a vendor's hosted reviewer — then a peer seam becomes real, and today that seam is served by nobody in this estate.

BAND is the wrong way to get there anyway, and the architectural tension is worth naming rather than leaving implicit. The estate's position is that **authority is singular** ([Ploeg ADR-0024](../../../ploeg/docs/adrs/0024-operator-work-uses-one-execution-authority.md)) and **the tracker stays authoritative**. A BAND room is a second place where work is discussed, a second identity system, a second retention policy and a second audit trail, hosted by a third party, with no way to bind a room decision to a Work Item revision or a candidate hash. The shape that survives the tension is the one [ADR-0007](../../../ploeg/docs/adrs/0007-a2a-adopt-nothing-watchlist-a-facade.md) already pre-authorised: a **separate north-facing service** that creates real tracker tickets and projects state back out, speaking a protocol with multi-vendor governance. That is A2A, already scoped as Ploeg backlog item 102, not BAND.

## 5. What to mine

Design ideas worth taking, with the acceptance test that would prove each one helps.

1. **Mention-scoped visibility.** Agents process only messages naming them; humans see everything. Our sequential crew dodges the context-blowup problem rather than solving it. *Test:* a bounded parallel crew ([PV-032](../../backlog/README.md)) where a reader sees only its briefing plus the artifacts addressed to it, and total tokens across the round stay under the sequential baseline.
2. **Named crew shapes.** BAND publishes eight: Assembly Line, Panel, Fan-out/Fan-in, Trip-wire Room, Runtime Recruit, Cross-boundary Crew, Breakout Room, Critic Overlay. Our defaults are two (`delivery`, `investigation`). *Test:* a versioned crew catalogue ([PV-029](../../backlog/README.md)) with at least Panel and Critic Overlay expressible without engine changes.
3. **The delete test.** Their meaningful-use bar: *"Take the room out. Does it still work?"* with load-bearing signals being dependent handoff, a runtime-decided roster, an enforced boundary and a blockable verdict. *Test:* every default crew must fail the equivalent — removing the reviewer must change a real outcome. Our governed-verdict gate at `engine.ts:753` already passes this; a purely advisory reviewer would not.
4. **Proxy-managed credential custody.** Jam's sandbox mixins let the guest see only the literal string `proxy-managed` while an egress proxy substitutes the real key, with a deny-by-default allowlist. Compare against [ADR-0011's relay](../adrs/0011-sandboxes-dial-out-through-a-relay.md): ours already dials out, but the harness still receives its inference credential. *Test:* a session where the workspace holds no model credential at all and the relay injects it.
5. **A per-message delegation exchange.** BAND's strongest primitive: a message-scoped grant traded for a provider token, bounded by a window, a rate limit and a per-message exchange cap, requiring the agent to have been addressed and the message to have crossed an ownership boundary. *Test:* nothing in our stack needs this today, but it is the right shape if a crew ever spans two owners. **Read AP2's open/closed mandate pair and `draft-nelson-agent-delegation-receipts-10` before designing ours** (§2) — between them they cover scope, time bound, budget cap, agent binding and revocation, and BAND's version covers only the first four.
6. **Two things to record as anti-patterns.** Do not make the chat room the system of record, and do not let version numbers signal stability — BAND cut three majors in ten weeks off single `!` commits while shipping a 1.0.0 whose only breaking change was a package rename.

## 6. Implementation path

**Phase 0, now, free.** Land this dossier, the ledger rows and the watchlist item. Fold ideas 1–3 into the existing tickets they belong to.

**Phase 1, only if a real need appears.** Snapshot BAND's OpenAPI into the repository if anyone ever integrates, because it is unversioned and served from a docs site, and re-check it on a cadence.

**Never:**

- Never add `@band-ai/sdk` to Vloer. It breaks [ADR-0002](../adrs/0002-native-node-and-single-writer-storage.md) and CI, and pulls two unauditable binaries from a private repository into a zero-dependency codebase.
- Never route Glide's coordination through `app.band.ai`. The audit trail is the product; sending it to a third-party SaaS with no self-host tier and no data-residency claim inverts the reason the estate is self-hosted.
- Never let a BAND room create work. A second front door beside the tracker violates the standing rule that the tracker stays authoritative.
- Never adopt BAND's chat-room-as-audit model in place of typed, signed candidates.
- Never treat BAND's framework list as an ecosystem. Every entry is an adapter BAND wrote.

## 7. The wider market

**The category BAND raised into has barely registered as a technique.** Thoughtworks Technology Radar carries no "agent mesh" and no "agent interaction layer" blip at all; A2A sits at **Assess**, *"worth exploring with the goal of understanding how it will affect your enterprise"*, which is not adopt and not trial. What the Radar does carry is *Coding Agent Swarms* in the caution ring — *"remain costly and are still far from mature... we advise caution when adopting this technique"* — and *MCP by Default*, also cautioned, with the argument that generalises the whole skeptical case: MCP imposes an *"abstraction tax"*, and *"a well-designed CLI with good `--help` output, structured JSON responses and predictable error handling often gives agents everything they need without the protocol overhead."* Ring-by-volume attribution was inconsistent across the per-blip pages and the Vol. 34 PDF could not be text-extracted, so **re-check the exact ring and volume before quoting these in a customer-facing document.**

**The market voted for the client seam, not the peer seam.** ACP has roughly 47 agents in its registry including Cursor, Devin, Copilot, Gemini, Codex, Claude, Goose, Junie and OpenCode. A2A, after a year and 150+ claimed member organisations, has two named GA product surfaces (Azure AI Foundry / Copilot Studio, and Bedrock AgentCore Runtime) by the Linux Foundation's own accounting, and has shipped no release since v1.0.1 on 28 May 2026. Developers wanted to plug an agent into an editor, not to have their agent phone another company's agent.

**MCP got smaller, not bigger.** The `2026-07-28` revision removed protocol-level sessions, removed the `initialize` handshake, deprecated Sampling with the migration path *"Integrate directly with LLM provider APIs"*, deprecated Logging in favour of OpenTelemetry, and demoted Tasks out of core into an extension that has zero clients on MCP's own support matrix. A standards body telling implementers to use the vendor API directly is the "you did not need the protocol for that" argument ratified by the protocol.

**The strongest critiques, and what they do and do not reach.** Walden Yan's [*Don't Build Multi-Agents*](https://cognition.com/blog/dont-build-multi-agents) argues that a subagent receives a task description but not the trace that produced it, so *"the decision-making ends up being too dispersed."* That lands squarely on @mention routing, which deliberately re-introduces the partial visibility he warns about. [Anthropic's multi-agent research post](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system) reports a 90.2% improvement over single-agent on research, alongside *"multi-agent systems use about 15× more tokens than chats"* and the explicit caveat that *"most coding tasks involve fewer truly parallelizable tasks than research"* — the vendor with the best result in the field says the pattern pays for research and not for coding, and BAND's flagship demo is coding agents. [Cemri et al., arXiv:2503.13657](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657) contributes MAST, 14 failure modes over 1,600+ annotated traces, and a whole category of it is *inter-agent misalignment* — the failure class an interaction layer claims to fix and which adding communication does not fix. None of these five critiques engages the governance claim, which remains BAND's most defensible ground and the half they themselves conceded.

**The sharpest MCP skeptic changed his mind, and the reason matters to us.** Simon Willison spent 2025 arguing that MCP had unfixable prompt-injection problems and that for coding agents *"the best possible tool for any situation is Bash."* On [31 July 2026](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/31/stateless-mcp/) he reversed on the strength of the stateless revision, and his new argument is a security argument against the bash-is-enough camp: *"Giving an agent a shell environment with the ability to access the internet is fraught with risk"*, whereas *"MCP tools are easier to audit and control, and simple enough that smaller models that run on a laptop can still drive them reasonably well."* Any "MCP is dead" framing is a 2025 artifact. This matters directly to [ADR-0012's refusal of terminals](../adrs/0012-agent-host-protocol-host.md) and to the `deny` list on `bash` and `edit` for read-only roles in [`src/runtime/opencode.ts`](../../src/runtime/opencode.ts): the industry's argument has moved toward our posture, not away from it.

**The strongest threat to harness portability is not a protocol, it is reinforcement learning.** Armin Ronacher's [*Better Models: Worse Tools*](https://lucumr.pocoo.org/2026/7/4/better-models-worse-tools/) (4 July 2026) documents newer Anthropic models *regressing* at tool-calling against non-Claude-Code schemas, diagnosing it as post-training inside a harness that tolerates slop through parameter aliases, silent key filtering and retry logic, so *"slightly malformed tool calls can still complete the task and receive reward."* His conclusion: *"tool schemas are not neutral, at least not on Anthropic models"*, and every other harness *"will have to inherit its quirks."* That is a de facto proprietary standard emerging through model weights rather than through a specification. It cannot be fixed by donating a protocol to a foundation, it is invisible to every governance-neutrality metric in the consolidation story, and it is a direct risk to [PV-031](../../backlog/README.md), our commitment to qualify a second open agent runtime. Note alongside it that **AGENTS.md, with 60,000+ projects, out-adopted every protocol in this survey, and it has no wire format at all.**

**"Agent mesh" is decaying as a category while BAND is selling into it.** [SolaceLabs/solace-agent-mesh](https://github.com/SolaceLabs/solace-agent-mesh), the most prominent open-source agent mesh, was **archived and made read-only on 17 September 2026**, the day before this survey. Solo.io dissolved its agent-mesh line by donating the pieces away: kagent and agentregistry to the CNCF, agentgateway to the Linux Foundation. The vendor category is being renamed to "AI gateway" and, in Gartner's August 2026 framing, "AI Agent Management Platforms". **BAND is not alone in its seam, and the occupant is Slack.** Agents are already @mentionable channel members on every Slack plan — *"add agents to channels, or DM with them one-on-one"*, with an Agents and tools tab in the sidebar. Slack's MCP server went GA on 17 February 2026, hosted at `mcp.slack.com/mcp` with **user-token OAuth so an agent inherits the authenticating user's permissions** rather than using a shared bot token, and Slack reports a 25× increase in tool calls since limited release. Admin approval gates every MCP server, write and delete tools require per-action user confirmation, and *"every AI action generates an audit log capturing the acting user, the action taken, and the relevant context."* That is BAND's surface, with better governance, inside a product every customer already has.

The structural difference is real but narrow: Slack routes hub-and-spoke through its own APIs with **no A2A, no agent-to-agent peer transport and no cross-organisation agent contacts**. Slack also has no per-agent cost attribution, no agent inventory and no agent control-plane SKU. So BAND's differentiators against the incumbent reduce to peer transport and cross-org contacts — two capabilities for which no demand has been demonstrated.

Two direct product analogues exist as startups: **Glue** (thread-based work chat, `@Glue` and `@Cursor` alongside your team, SOC 2, Slack history import, SaaS only, no MCP or A2A) and **Teamily AI** (human-and-agent workspace, publicly launched 8 July 2026). Neither publishes pricing or protocol support. And the coordination *thesis* did attract enormous money at a different altitude: **Humans& raised a $480M seed at $4.5B on 20 January 2026** to build long-horizon multi-agent coordination models. So the earlier framing in this dossier — that nobody funded BAND's proposition — was too strong. What is true is narrower and still decision-relevant: **no company found combines agents as room members, peer-to-peer protocol transport, and per-agent cost attribution.** Glue and Teamily have the surface without the protocols or the cost layer; the governance startups have the cost layer without the surface.

**The 2026 money says warning.** What the incumbents actually bought was identity and gateways: CrowdStrike acquired SGNL for $740M, Cisco acquired Astrix Security (announced 4 May, closed 29 June 2026, around $400M), Okta acquired Permiso (~$200M), SailPoint acquired Entro (~$200M), Snowflake acquired Natoma, Palo Alto acquired Portkey, and Stripe is reported to be acquiring OpenRouter for $7B+. What got the largest independent round was durable execution: **Temporal raised $550M at $12.55B on 14 September 2026**, against 4,300+ paying customers and $250M+ ARR. Vertical agent applications with checkable customer lists raised heavily too. **The horizontal, neutral coordination layer raised almost nothing and shipped no verifiable customers.**

**And the one company that did ship a horizontal agent OS to the public lasted thirty-five days.** Dreamer, formerly /dev/agents, launched an open beta on 18 February 2026 on a $56M seed at roughly a $500M valuation; Meta acqui-hired the entire founding team on 23 March 2026. Letta made the same retreat more quietly: on 16 March 2026 it refocused on a model-agnostic CLI harness and **sunset its hosted platform entirely**. Two independent retreats from hosted agent infrastructure into a local harness, in the same month, plus BAND's own Codeband retreat — that is a pattern rather than an anecdote.

**BAND has the weakest evidence-to-capital ratio in the field.** Its funding announcement names no customer and no design partner, only *"early adopters in software development, enterprise automation, and advanced R&D"*. Five months on, band.ai shows **no customer logos, no case studies and no usage numbers**; the agent tiles on the home page are templates rather than deployments; every call to action is "Book a Demo".

**Gartner's own 2026 position is a direct challenge to the category.** Its May 2026 prediction is that *applying uniform governance across AI agents will lead to enterprise AI agent failure* — the single-control-plane policy is itself named as the failure mode. Gartner does not use "agentic mesh" at all. The term that actually won 2026 is **"agent control plane"**, and no analyst has ratified a single vendor name for it; "agent interaction layer" is essentially BAND's own coinage.

**The structural argument against the whole bet.** The protocols such a layer would coordinate are being standardised for free, in public, by AWS, Google, Microsoft, Cisco, IBM, Salesforce, SAP and ServiceNow: MCP, goose and AGENTS.md into the Agentic AI Foundation in December 2025, A2A into AAIF in August 2026, AP2 to the FIDO Alliance in April 2026, x402 to the Linux Foundation in April 2026. A venture-funded startup selling the coordination layer is competing with a foundation giving it away.

**BAND's closest architectural analogue is free and Apache-2.0.** AG2 v1.0 (released 27 July 2026, shipping roughly monthly since) replaced its classic group chat with **Network**: a hub holding a registry, an audit log, typed channels and governance rules, where agents find each other by name and capability and talk over a pluggable transport — same-process queues by default, or WebSocket with each agent on a separate host. It speaks MCP, A2A with signed agent cards, and ACP. What it does not have is BAND's humans-as-participants model, the bilateral Contacts consent graph, or a hosted control plane. Anyone evaluating BAND's mesh should price it against a library that already does the routing part for nothing. Note too that **LangGraph Platform ships a real A2A surface** at `/a2a/{assistant_id}` with agent-card discovery at `/.well-known/agent-card.json`, so A2A adoption is broader than the two hyperscaler surfaces the Linux Foundation could name.

**The serious long-run competitor is not BAND.** Microsoft Foundry Agent Service now runs hosted agents in your own container across Agent Framework, LangGraph, the OpenAI Agents SDK, the Anthropic Agent SDK and the GitHub Copilot SDK; gives **each hosted agent a dedicated Entra identity**; curates tools behind a single managed MCP-compatible endpoint; ships A2A v1.0 GA; and distributes through Teams, M365 Copilot and the Entra Agent Registry. Entra Agent ID is GA through Agent 365. Inside a Microsoft tenant that is the most complete first-party stack in this landscape. It documents **no per-agent budget caps**.

### The workbench category, refreshed

The [9 September shortlist](market-landscape.md#4-shortlist-products-that-should-influence-the-decision) needs two corrections and one warning.

**Kandev was understated.** It is not parallel sessions with a board. It ships an inter-agent FIFO message bus with interrupt semantics, parent and child workflow gates, explicit profile handoffs, and a per-session Kubernetes Pod executor with namespaced RBAC across six trackers — AGPL, one Go binary, no telemetry, weekly releases through v0.94.0 on 9 September. Coordination and workspace placement are substantively covered. It has no budgets, no spend attribution, no provenance, no ACP and no documented replay. At 804 stars with no company behind it, it is an architectural threat rather than a commercial one: a determined team could bolt LiteLLM onto it in a month.

**OpenHands Enterprise is the closest funded version of this thesis.** Self-hosted on your own Kubernetes via Helm, Sysbox-isolated sandboxes, SAML and Keycloak RBAC, multi-user organisations, **budgeting through LiteLLM — the same gateway we use** — per-run cost display, ACP in both directions, and Jira Cloud plus Data Center. It shipped twelve releases in August and September. A prospect who writes down the requirements and shops them will get a doc URL for most lines. The gaps that remain are precise: per-task budget with a virtual-key lifecycle attributed to a ticket, declared reviewer crews, provenance, and per-customer tenancy.

**The attrition is the story.** Terragon shut down in February 2026. Vibe Kanban is sunsetting with 28.1k stars. Continue was acquired and archived. Coder removed Tasks in 2.37. Sculptor has been silent since April. Meanwhile a ~250-entry census of the category exists, almost all of it "N isolated agents in git worktrees plus a dashboard", and the largest open-source entry by an order of magnitude (~71,400 stars) is exactly that pattern. **Stars are not survival: 28k did not save Vibe Kanban.**

### The empty columns

Checked against each product's own documentation. Nothing in the category does any of these.

1. **Artifact provenance or signing.** Zero products document in-toto, SLSA or signed commits on agent-produced candidates. The closest anyone gets is audit *logs of what happened* rather than attestations verifiable off-platform. This is [ADR-0014](../adrs/0014-signed-candidates.md), and it is the single most defensible position available.
2. **Per-task budget with spend attributed to the originating ticket.** Every budget in the category is per-user, per-organisation or per-agent, on a monthly or lifetime cycle. Devin comes closest with per-run and per-repository cost visibility, and still caps at the org. **Nobody binds a budget to a task, mints a scoped credential for it, and revokes it when the task closes.** That is Ploeg's Inference Account, and it is a whole product axis with no occupant.
3. **Event-sourced durable sessions with replay as a user-visible contract.** Resume and persistence are common; *replay* appears once in ~250 catalogued projects, as a primitive rather than a product.
4. **Reusable reviewer crews as a declarable object.** Coordination exists — handoffs, gates, coordinator-and-subagent trees. Declared, reusable review structure does not.
5. **Per-customer tenancy for an agency:** several clients, separate forges, trackers, budgets and data boundaries in one installation. Only Paperclip documents multi-company isolation, and Paperclip does not do software delivery.
6. **A self-hosted control plane from any commercial vendor.** Cursor self-hosts execution and keeps orchestration. Factory self-hosts the compute and keeps the platform. **Self-hosting the control plane remains an open-source-only property.** That is a licence moat, not a technical one.

Five of those six are Glide's existing design. That is the clearest strategic signal in this survey, and it is worth more than anything BAND-specific in it.

### The finding that cuts against us

**ACP has won the editor seam, and AHP has no third-party adoption to attach to.** OpenHands is an ACP client *and* exposes IDE-over-ACP; BAND ships both ACP server and client adapters; Zed, Claude Code and Gemini CLI all ship ACP entrypoints; roughly 47 agents sit in the ACP registry. Across this entire workbench survey, **AHP appears zero times outside our own repository**. The blunt version from the sweep: *an AHP host that does not also speak ACP is attaching to an empty seam.*

This does not invalidate [ADR-0012](../adrs/0012-agent-host-protocol-host.md). Its actual justification is that VS Code will attach to any host listed in `chat.remoteAgentHosts`, and that remains true and remains free. But the ADR's implicit hope — that other clients arrive — now has evidence against it, and the estate's ACP position is asymmetric: Ploeg speaks ACP south through `pkg/harness/adapters/acp`, while Vloer's north surface is AHP alone. If a second editor surface is ever worth building, the evidence says ACP, not more AHP. Recorded as a trigger in §10 rather than as a reversal.

### Does any of this actually work

The strongest published evidence, [arXiv 2609.13890](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13890) (12 September 2026), evaluates five communication topologies over 614 problems with a budget-matched protocol and a maths replication. Hierarchical multi-agent collaboration beats a single agent by **+2.4 points of pass@1 on the easiest third of problems and +21.1 on the hardest third, at roughly ten times the token cost throughout**. A difficulty-aware router reaches 77.7% pass@1 at 40% of always-hierarchical cost, beating always-hierarchical at 73.6%; all eleven pairwise comparisons survive Holm-Bonferroni correction.

Read against our product: **running a crew on every task burns ten times the tokens to buy 2.4 points on easy work.** The value is real and concentrated in hard tasks, and capturing it needs difficulty-aware routing plus per-task cost accounting — which is an independent argument for the budget axis nobody occupies. It is also a caution against making crews the default for everything.

Worth recording alongside it: [METR's February 2026 update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/) retracted the confidence behind its own widely-cited "AI made tasks 19% slower" result, reporting raw speedups in the successor study while saying the signal is unreliable because developers refuse to participate without AI and withheld 30–50% of tasks they expected AI to do well. METR has published no productivity result since. **As of today there is no rigorous trial measuring multiple coding agents against one.** Anyone claiming otherwise, in either direction, is ahead of the evidence.

**Not established.** The session's web-search budget was exhausted early, so later work ran on direct fetches of primary pages. Specific items that remain **unverified rather than absent**, and should not be repeated without a better source: Gartner's Hype Cycle for Agentic AI 2026 innovation profiles (gartner.com blocks automated fetch); the Stripe/OpenRouter acquisition, which is reported and not confirmed closed; the Anthropic/Stainless and Cisco/Astrix price tags, which come from secondary reporting rather than filings; Thoughtworks ring-by-volume attribution; and Google's *"FinOps for the AI era: new flexible billing and cost controls for agents"*, whose title is index-verified but whose article 404'd on fetch. That last one is the highest-value open gap in this survey, because **if anyone has shipped per-agent budget enforcement at hyperscaler scale, that is where it would be.**

## 8. What the future looks like

Clearly labelled as extrapolation from the dated evidence above, not as sourced prediction.

1. **ACP standardises the editor-to-agent seam.** It has the adoption, two-vendor governance (Zed and JetBrains, with lead-maintainer vetoes), a public meetings repository and a stated intent to move to a foundation. The signal to watch is whether Streamable HTTP graduates from its transports working group, because that is the moment ACP starts competing with AHP's remote story.
2. **AHP stays single-vendor through 2027.** One host implementation, a public extension API declared a non-goal on 14 September 2026, and a wire-breaking minor cadence. Treat 0.9.x as a compatibility treadmill rather than a platform. The `chat.remoteAgentHosts` setting stays open because it is VS Code's own test harness.
3. **MCP going stateless vacates the session layer rather than filling it.** This is the strongest structural argument that a host and session layer is durable rather than about to be absorbed, and it supports [ADR-0012](../adrs/0012-agent-host-protocol-host.md) more than it threatens it.
4. **Discovery converges first,** through the AI Catalog, a Linux Foundation working repository built by members of the MCP and A2A communities, whose adoption the two steering committees are due to vote on. That vote is the nearest dated decision point in the landscape.
5. **Cost, authority, provenance and delivery stay unclaimed, and this is deliberate rather than accidental.** This is the most useful finding in the sweep and it is not extrapolation. MCP co-creator David Soria Parra, speaking at MCP Dev Summit NA in April 2026: *"MCP has a defined purpose: connecting AI applications to data sources. Observability belongs to OpenTelemetry. Identity belongs to dedicated projects."* The Agentic AI Foundation chose the Kubernetes model over owning the stack, and every other data point is consistent with it — MCP deprecating its own Logging in favour of OpenTelemetry, AHP making its telemetry channel a bare OTLP pass-through, and `draft-ietf-wimse-aims-00` composing WIMSE, OAuth 2.0 and SPIFFE rather than inventing an identity system. The ecosystem's design principle is to compose external standards at the boundary and own nothing above the wire. **A product that holds execution authority, budgets, leases and evidence is not late to a standard; it is operating in a space the standards bodies have explicitly declined to enter.**
6. **Identity is the one unclaimed-adjacent layer that is actively closing.** `draft-ietf-wimse-aims-00` was adopted by the IETF WIMSE working group on **15 September 2026**, three days before this survey — the first working-group-adopted agent-identity document. It covers human-to-agent delegation via an OAuth authorization-code grant with explicit user approval, short-lived credentials and revocation through the OpenID Shared Signals Framework. It has **no budget concept**, and translating a mission into authorization requirements is explicitly out of scope. Web Bot Auth also became a chartered IETF working group, answering which bot this is and never what it may spend.
7. **Specs are starting to compose rather than compete, and the proof is in production.** The Universal Commerce Protocol (Google and Shopify, live in Google AI Mode and the Gemini app) binds to MCP, A2A *and* plain REST, uses OAuth 2.0 for account linking, and integrates AP2 as its payment-mandate layer. It is the first real example of a specification treating MCP and A2A as transports rather than rivals, which is the layering thesis working rather than being argued.

## 9. Recommendations

1. **Record BAND as rejected-and-mined** in the product ledger and the protocol appendix. Do not open an integration ticket.
2. **Do not reopen the agent-to-agent question on BAND's account.** [Ploeg ADR-0007](../../../ploeg/docs/adrs/0007-a2a-adopt-nothing-watchlist-a-facade.md) holds and is strengthened: A2A's lack of lease and queue semantics is now confirmed by direct spec grep rather than inferred.
3. **Honour ADR-0006 and ADR-0007's `review-by: 2026-10-31`** rather than rolling it. Both have new evidence, recorded in the amendments below.
4. **Fold the three design ideas into existing tickets** (PV-032, PV-029, and the crew-design gate) rather than creating new ones.
5. **Stop treating "no standard for budget and provenance" as a gap.** This sweep establishes it as an unclaimed layer with nobody working toward it. It is a differentiator to market, not a debt to repay.
6. **Re-run the unearned sections** with a fresh search budget before any commercial positioning work depends on the competitive picture.

## 10. Re-evaluation triggers

Any one of these reopens this record. Each is checkable in minutes.

- **BAND publishes a self-hostable server artifact** — an image, a chart, or a licence to run the Elixir backend. This single change would invalidate most of §1.
- **A third party ships a BAND integration BAND did not write.** Watch the dependents graphs for `band-sdk` and `@band-ai/sdk`, both at zero today.
- **Jam ships code delivery** — a diff, a PR, or a merge gate of its own — reversing the Codeband retreat.
- **BAND's OpenAPI grows spend, provenance or repository vocabulary.** All at zero today; the document is at [docs.band.ai/openapi.json](https://docs.band.ai/openapi.json) and takes one grep.
- **Any standards body charters a working group for cost, authority, provenance or delivery.** None exists across MCP's 11 working groups and 7 interest groups, A2A's TSC, or AHP — and per §8.5 this is a stated scope boundary, so a charter would be a genuine reversal rather than a gap closing.
- **FIDO's Agentic Authentication Technical Working Group publishes anything carrying a revocation story.** That is where a delegated-authority credential with a cap, a window and a kill switch could actually land, and revocation is the one property [AP2 v0.2](https://ap2-protocol.org/) lacks.
- **`draft-nelson-agent-delegation-receipts` is working-group adopted**, or `draft-ietf-wimse-aims` grows a budget concept. The receipts draft is Ploeg's evidence model written as an Internet-Draft; adoption would turn a differentiator into a standard worth conforming to.
- **AHP cuts 0.10.0.** Twenty-one days of silence after a 10-to-18-day cadence is the longest gap in the series, and a minor bump breaks our wire by AHP's own compatibility rule.
- **The AI Catalog adoption vote lands** at the MCP and A2A steering committees — the first formal cross-specification merge decision in the landscape.
- **Thoughtworks Radar Vol. 35 (around November 2026) moves A2A off Assess** in either direction, or a named metric-backed A2A production deployment appears outside the cloud-platform set. Note that no frontier lab ships A2A today: every named adopter is a cloud or SaaS platform, and the labs are where coding agents come from.
- **A2A grows queue or pub-sub transport** ([a2aproject/A2A#1029](https://github.com/a2aproject/A2A/issues/1029)) — already a trigger on ADR-0007, restated because it is the one change that would make a peer protocol an executor-seam candidate.
- **A real peer arrives**: a client's or vendor's agent that must join a crew without running on our infrastructure. That reopens §4, and the answer is still the A2A facade rather than BAND.
- **Any third-party AHP client attaches to Vloer's host**, or conversely, **a second editor surface becomes worth building**. On today's evidence the second case argues for ACP rather than more AHP, because ACP has roughly 47 registered agents and AHP has no adoption outside this repository. Ploeg already speaks ACP south; Vloer speaking ACP north would be the new work, and it should be justified by a named client rather than by symmetry.
- **OpenHands Enterprise ships per-task budgets, declared reviewer crews, or candidate provenance.** It already self-hosts on Kubernetes and already budgets through LiteLLM, so these are the only remaining gaps between it and this product. Any one of them closing is a direct competitive event.

## 11. Amendments to standing records

New evidence for existing bets, recorded here rather than left to be rediscovered.

- **[Ploeg ADR-0006](../../../ploeg/docs/adrs/0006-ahp-is-the-wrong-layer.md) (AHP is the wrong layer), review-by 2026-10-31.** All three drivers hold or strengthen. Single vendor confirmed: no TSC, no charter, no foundation, CODEOWNERS added only on 2026-09-16. Cadence is decelerating but still wire-breaking, with 21 days since 0.9.0 (2026-08-28) — the longest gap in the series. The layer argument is unchanged.
- **[Vloer ADR-0012](../adrs/0012-agent-host-protocol-host.md) and [the 2026-09-17 roadmap sweep](2026-09-17-agent-host-roadmap.md).** Two facts need reconciling rather than correcting. `Qusic/pi-ahp` and `softov/ahpd` both exist and resolve today, so "independent implementations ship" is accurate. Microsoft's [implementations page](https://microsoft.github.io/agent-host-protocol/guide/implementations.html) lists exactly one server — *"VS Code agent host — The reference AHP server implementation"* — and no third-party host at all. **Independent hosts exist and the spec vendor does not acknowledge them.** That is a sharper statement of the single-vendor risk than either version alone, and it should replace the current wording in both records. Separately, the official client list is Rust, TypeScript, Kotlin, Swift and Go plus AHPX; the `.NET` client named in [the 2026-09-10 research](2026-09-10-agent-host-protocol.md) does not appear on it.
- **AHP's extension namespace was settled** as the `x-` prefix ([#367](https://github.com/microsoft/agent-host-protocol/issues/367), closed 2026-08-13 with zero comments). This is the spec-level answer to the `vscode/`-prefixed private methods observed in the shipped build, and it is not yet recorded in our research.
- **[Ploeg ADR-0007](../../../ploeg/docs/adrs/0007-a2a-adopt-nothing-watchlist-a-facade.md) (A2A), review-by 2026-10-31.** Facts hold, with two updates: A2A moved from Linux Foundation top-level into the Agentic AI Foundation as a Growth Stage project on 2026-08-27, and has shipped no release since v1.0.1 on 2026-05-28. Its core reasoning is now confirmed rather than inferred: a direct grep of the full spec finds zero leasing or exactly-once semantics and only `MAY`-level idempotency, meaning a retried send can legitimately double-execute. The TSC's "Steady State" transition is due around December 2026 with the election method still undecided.
- **AHP [#266](https://github.com/microsoft/agent-host-protocol/issues/266)** — the proposal to isolate privileged operations and approvals, which ADR-0012 cites as the unresolved threat model behind declining terminals and resource writes — remains open, unlabeled, unmilestoned and dormant since 2026-07-05. A commenter proposed a receipt chain of `payload_hash` / `approved_hash` / `policy_hash` / `execution_receipt` on the principle that *"host-only success is not authoritative"*. **This is the closest anything in the ecosystem gets to Ploeg's execution-authority model, and it is stalled.** The decision to decline terminals and resource writes stands.

## 12. Method and limits

Nine research agents covering BAND's documentation, its repositories and SDKs, its competitors, the standards landscape, the coding-agent workbench category, and this repository's own seams. Claims about BAND's API surface, the existence of `Qusic/pi-ahp` and `softov/ahpd`, and the contents of the AHP implementations page were verified first-hand rather than taken from an agent.

What this does not establish: BAND was not deployed, installed or benchmarked. Its documented behaviour is not an independently verified security guarantee, and neither is anyone else's. Download statistics and dependency graphs are proxies for adoption, not measurements of use; the code-search leg of the adoption check failed on rate limits, so the zero-external-dependents finding rests on two dependency indexes rather than a grep. The competitive and funding sections are explicitly incomplete for the reasons given in §7. The extrapolation in §8 is labelled as such throughout.
