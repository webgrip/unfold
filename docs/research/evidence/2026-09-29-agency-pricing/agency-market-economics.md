# Agency market economics and buying behaviour (NL/EU, 2025-2026)

Research method note (2026-09-29): all primary pages (emerce.nl, dutchdigitalagencies.com, prometheanresearch.com) were blocked by the network egress proxy, so figures below come from search-engine summaries of those pages, not full-text reads. Each figure is attributed to the page the search result pointed to. Treat exact numbers as "reported by search snippet" and verify against the original before publishing them in a pitch. Estimates and inferences are marked as such.

## 1. Number and size distribution of web/software/digital agencies (NL, EU)

### Takeaway
There is no official count of "digital agencies". NL has ~106-108k IT-services businesses (SBI 62, dominated by one-person businesses), of which ~46k are registered as software development; the organised agency tier is small: the Dutch Digital Agencies (DDA) benchmark covers 144 agencies with ~7,700 FTE and ~EUR 1.2bn revenue. The realistic Glide target (agencies with an in-house dev team of roughly 5-80 FTE) is likely in the low thousands in NL (estimate).

### Cited Findings
- CBS: the number of ICT companies in NL grew in 2025 to "just over 106 thousand" (all ICT, broad definition, includes sole traders) — [CBS, Digitalisering en kenniseconomie 2025 (published 2026)](https://www.cbs.nl/nl-nl/longread/rapportages/2026/digitalisering-en-kenniseconomie-2025?onepage=true)
- CBS switches to the SBI2025 classification from 2025 — same source as above.
- 107,545 businesses registered under SBI 62 (IT services) in 2026; 45,680 active under SBI 62.01 (software development); 8,300 under SBI 62.09 (other IT services) — [CompanyData, SBI 62 (2026), aggregator of KVK data](https://companydata.com/nl/sbi-code/sbi-code-62/). Aggregator, not KVK itself; KVK notes such breakdowns are not available as open data — [data.overheid.nl request](https://data.overheid.nl/community/datarequest/alle-bedrijven-in-sbi-62-dienstverlenende-activiteiten-op-het-gebied-van-informatietechnologie-en-sb)
- DDA Benchmark 2024 (published June 2025): 144 Dutch digital agencies; total FTE fell from ~8,390 to ~7,701 (-7% average FTE), while total revenue rose ~3% — [Dutch IT Channel](https://www.dutchitchannel.nl/research/656222/dutch-digital-agencies-benchmark-2024-winstgevendheid-blijft-overeind-in-uitdagende-markt); [DDA publication](https://dutchdigitalagencies.com/publicaties/dutch-digital-agencies-benchmark-2024-winstgevendheid-blijft-overeind-in-uitdagen/)

### Inferences
- (Estimate) DDA benchmark agencies average ~53 FTE (7,701 / 144) and ~EUR 8.3m revenue (1.2bn / 144). This is the upper, organised segment.
- (Estimate) The Simplicate customer base (~550 agencies) plus DDA members (~150) gives a floor of several hundred professionally run agencies in NL; the SBI 62.01 population (~46k) is overwhelmingly freelancers. A reasonable NL serviceable market for Glide is probably 1,000-3,000 agencies with 3+ developers; this needs validating (e.g. KVK paid extract of SBI 62.01/73.11 by employee class).
- The "5-80 FTE" band used by Emerce is a natural definition of the Glide core segment.

## 2. Hourly rates, billable utilization, margins, revenue per FTE (NL)

### Takeaway
Dutch agencies bill on average ~EUR 105/hour (Q3 2025), with a soft ceiling around EUR 150-160 where clients push back; only ~55% of time is productive/billable; revenue per FTE is ~EUR 160k (top performers ~EUR 234k); net margins internationally average ~13%. Billable hours, not rate, are the main constraint.

### Cited Findings
- Average agency hourly rate EUR 105 (Q3 2025), up from EUR 103 in Q1 2025; nearly 550 NL creative/digital/marketing agencies (Simplicate Agencies Benchmark Q3 2025); headline says "half of the time non-productive" — [Emerce](https://www.emerce.nl/nieuws/105-euro-per-uur-helft-tijd-nietproductief)
- Digital agencies average ~55% productivity vs 70%+ for architects and accountants — [Emerce AI/agencies coverage](https://www.emerce.nl/nieuws/onderzoek-invloed-ai-digital-agencies-kosten-kunnen-verviervoudigen) (search summary; exact page attribution uncertain between Emerce articles)
- Earlier Simplicate data: agency productivity only 54% — [MarketingTribune, 2024-01](https://www.marketingtribune.nl/bureaus/nieuws/2024/01/onderzoek-productiviteit-bij-agencies-slechts-54/index.xml)
- Digital agencies and online marketing agencies averaged EUR 98.20/hour in 2023 — [Fonk](https://fonkmagazine.nl/artikelen/bureaus/gemiddeld-uurtarief-agencies-stijgt-maar-onderlinge-verschillen-zijn-groot-67470.html)
- Emerce Tarieven Digital Agencies 2025 (published ~Nov 2025): a small group consistently charges >EUR 150/hour; agencies notice clients become critical as rates approach EUR 160; 73% expect another (more moderate) rate increase in 2026; report covers junior/medior/senior rates, project prices and revenue models — [Emerce](https://www.emerce.nl/nieuws/onderzoek-tarieven-digital-agencies-tariefgrens-schuift-hogere-prijzen-stuiten-klantkritiek); [report page](https://www.emerce.nl/knowledgebase/emerce-rapport-tarieven-digital-agencies-2025)
- Average rate of agencies with 5-80 FTE grew 7.3% (year not visible in snippet) — [Emerce](https://www.emerce.nl/nieuws/gemiddeld-uurtarief-agencies-580-fte-groeit-73-procent)
- Freelance benchmarks 2025: zzp software developer avg EUR 92/hour; zzp web developer avg EUR 71/hour (excl. VAT) — [Knab (software dev)](https://bieb.knab.nl/ondernemen/wat-verdient-een-software-developer-zzp-bekijk-uurtarief-en-winst-in-2025); [Knab (web dev)](https://bieb.knab.nl/ondernemen/wat-verdient-een-web-developer-zzp-bekijk-uurtarief-en-winst-in-2025)
- Local IT secondment (detachering) 2025: EUR 65-150/hour — [Orange Melons](https://www.orangemelons.com/wat-is-een-redelijk-tarief-voor-een-gedetacheerde-developer-in-nederland/) (vendor blog)
- DDA Benchmark 2024: average revenue per FTE ~EUR 160k (+~10% YoY); high performers ~EUR 234k per FTE; two-thirds of high performers are small or medium agencies; "margins under pressure" but profitability held — [Dutch IT Channel](https://www.dutchitchannel.nl/research/656222/dutch-digital-agencies-benchmark-2024-winstgevendheid-blijft-overeind-in-uitdagende-markt)
- Simplicate (consultancy sector, for comparison): billability Q3 2024 57%, top 20% at 73% — [Consultancy.nl](https://www.consultancy.nl/nieuws/57722/verschil-in-declarabiliteit-tussen-bureaus-nam-toe-in-derde-kwartaal)
- International (mostly US) net margins: Promethean Research 2026 State of Digital Services (119 agency leaders, Feb 2026): average after-tax net margin 13% in 2025 vs 15% long-run average since 2015; <10 employees averaged 19%, 50+ employees 8%; agencies that reduced services grew 13% and posted 30% net margins — [Promethean Research](https://prometheanresearch.com/2026-state-of-digital-services-digital-agency-industry-research/)
- Promethean 2025 report: 36% of US digital agencies charged $175-199/hr, 32% $200-249/hr; 28% raised prices 2024->2025 — [Promethean 2025 report](https://prometheanresearch.com/2025-digital-agency-industry-report/) (via search summary)
- Productive "State of the Agency Business 2025": 59% of agencies grew revenue but only 31% improved margins; fewer than half operate above 10% margin; nearly one in five cannot report their margin — [Productive](https://productive.io/reports/agency-industry-report/)
- Utilization target 65-80% commonly cited; Promethean recommends 72% for production teams — [search summary citing TMetric/Promethean](https://blog.tmetric.com/marketing-agency-profitability-benchmarks/)

### Inferences
- (Estimate) Effective revenue per hour worked at a Dutch agency ~ EUR 105 x 55% ≈ EUR 58. A developer costing an agency ~EUR 60-75k/year fully loaded (not sourced; typical NL salary + ~30% on-costs) produces ~EUR 160k revenue, consistent with the DDA figure.
- The ~EUR 160/hour client resistance point means agencies cannot grow margin by rate alone; margin must come from fixed-price/productized delivery where cost falls (AI) but price does not. This is the core Glide pitch.
- Implication for credit pricing (estimate): a small ticket that takes 2-4 billable hours at EUR 105 is worth EUR 210-420 to the end client; Glide's per-ticket cost must leave the agency a clearly larger gross margin than the ~45-55% implied by hourly delivery.

### Gaps

- NL-specific net/EBITDA margin figures from DDA (the benchmark text says "margins under pressure" without a number in the snippet).
- Split between dev-heavy (web/software) agencies and marketing agencies in the Simplicate rate average.

## 3. Pricing models: hourly, fixed-price, retainers, strippenkaarten, maintenance, productized; shift from hourly due to AI

### Takeaway
Agencies mix models (time & materials, fixed bid, retainer); few use only one. In NL, strippenkaarten (prepaid hour bundles, typically 10-100 hours, 1-year validity, 15-minute increments) and monthly maintenance contracts are standard for small changes, which is exactly the ticket type Glide automates. Dutch research expects a shift from hourly to result-based pricing because of AI, but ~40% of agencies do not yet know how.

### Cited Findings
- Most digital agencies mix time & materials, fixed bid and retainer; 4% or fewer rely on a single model exclusively — [Promethean 2025 report (search summary)](https://prometheanresearch.com/2025-digital-agency-industry-report/)
- Value-based pricing firms ~18% net margin vs ~13% for hourly-billed agencies — [Agiled statistics roundup](https://agiled.app/statistics/agency-pricing-statistics) (aggregator; original source not confirmed)
- Emerce: because of AI, agencies will increasingly move from hourly models to result-based pricing; the time-based model is under pressure; nearly 40% of agencies do not yet know how AI will affect prices and business models; about one-third expect higher rates due to increased value — [Emerce, invloed AI op digital agencies](https://www.emerce.nl/nieuws/onderzoek-invloed-ai-digital-agencies-kosten-kunnen-verviervoudigen); [Emerce, resultaatgerichte prijsmodellen in opkomst](https://www.emerce.nl/nieuws/onderzoek-ai-digital-agencies-media-performance-onder-druk-resultaatgerichte-prijsmodellen-opkomst); [Emerce report De Impact van AI op Digital Agencies (2026)](https://www.emerce.nl/knowledgebase/emerce-rapport-impact-van-ai-op-digital-agencies-2025)
- An Emerce study headline states that AI-related costs for agencies "can quadruple" — [Emerce](https://www.emerce.nl/nieuws/onderzoek-invloed-ai-digital-agencies-kosten-kunnen-verviervoudigen) (headline only; detail not retrieved)
- Strippenkaart practice: bundles of 20, 50 or 100 hours with lower rate for larger bundles; hours deducted in 15-minute blocks; hours valid 1 year (some never expire, non-refundable); paid in advance — [Custom Website](https://www.customwebsite.nl/website-onderhoud/strippenkaart/); [De Onliners](https://deonliners.nl/informatie/strippenkaart)
- Example strippenkaart prices: 4 h EUR 320 / 8 h EUR 640 (EUR 80/h); 10 h EUR 720 without expiry — [search summary of NL provider pages, e.g. Boekhout-Multimedia](https://www.boekhout-multimedia.nl/onze-diensten/service-onderhouds-contract-op-uw-website/website-onderhoud-service-contracten-strippenkaart)
- Monthly website maintenance packages from EUR 25-37.50/month (small providers) — [NoLimitDesign 2026](https://nolimitdesign.nl/inspiratiehub/website-onderhoud-bespaar-kosten-met-slim-onderhoud/); [De Webtuin](https://www.dewebtuin.nl/technisch-website-onderhoud/)

### Inferences
- The strippenkaart is a ready-made mental model for Glide credits: agencies already sell prepaid units that are consumed per task. A Glide credit bundle maps cleanly onto "tickets" instead of "hours", and the agency can reprice a strippenkaart as a "ticketkaart" (e.g. per small change) without the client seeing hours.
- (Estimate) Low-end maintenance (EUR 25-40/month) is commoditised and not the target; the value is in the change-request tier currently billed at EUR 80-150/hour in 15-minute increments.

### Gaps
- Share of NL agency revenue by pricing model (hourly vs fixed vs retainer) — likely in the Emerce 2025 rates report or DDA benchmark but not retrievable.
- Evidence of Dutch agencies already selling "per ticket" or productized dev subscriptions (e.g. "unlimited requests" models) was not searched in depth.

## 4. AI coding tool adoption and AI's effect on agency billing and margins

### Takeaway
AI use is near universal among Dutch agencies, but monetisation is unsettled. Surveys show clients asking for "AI discounts" (about a third) yet few agencies (13%) have cut prices; agencies that keep prices and cut internal time see the biggest margin gains. Among developers, Claude Code and Cursor are the leading primary tools.

### Cited Findings
- Nearly all Dutch agencies use AI to speed up work, which pressures the time-based business model — [Emerce](https://www.emerce.nl/nieuws/omzet-nederlandse-digital-agencies-12-miljard-euro) (search summary)
- Emerce salary and job-satisfaction survey Digital Agencies 2026: "AI leaves its mark on worries and satisfaction" — [Emerce](https://www.emerce.nl/nieuws/salaris-werktevredenheidsonderzoek-digital-agencies-2026-ai-druk-stempel-zorgen-tevredenheid) (headline only)
- Emerce Agency Radar 2026: agencies invest "on three levels at once" — [Emerce](https://www.emerce.nl/nieuws/onderzoek-agency-radar-2026) (headline only)
- Productive (2025, 180+ agencies): about a third have received client requests for an "AI discount", about half expect to; only 13% have actually lowered prices; 65% say AI boosts revenue or profitability — [Productive, Agencies in the AI Era (2025)](https://productive.io/blog/agencies-in-the-ai-era/) (via search summary / [Technology.org 2026-04-23](https://www.technology.org/2026/04/23/inside-the-productive-io-survey-what-ai-agencies-actually-charge-in-2026/))
- Productive "Agencies in the AI Era 2.0" pulse (174 agencies, 17-31 March 2026; 54% Europe, 61% executives, skewed to 20-50 employees): discount requests did not grow over six months; firms with biggest margin gains kept prices steady while using AI to cut production time; 52% of agencies with multiple positive AI outcomes keep or raise prices with improved margins (up from 47%) — [Productive](https://productive.io/reports/agencies-in-the-ai-era-pulse-report/)
- Promethean 2026 (Feb 2026, 119 leaders): 34% implemented AI across the business, 28% actively implementing; AI maturity measured across coding, design, copy, marketing, ops, PM, sales, video — [Promethean](https://prometheanresearch.com/2026-state-of-digital-services-digital-agency-industry-research/)
- Clients increasingly expect cheaper services due to AI, contributing to margin compression (13% vs 15% long-run) — [Promethean 2025 report via Agiled](https://agiled.app/statistics/agency-pricing-statistics) (aggregator)
- Developer survey 2026: Claude Code (28%) and Cursor (24%) are over half of primary-tool picks; cost volatility became the top concern as per-token/per-request agentic pricing made monthly bills swing 2-3x quarter over quarter — [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/ai-coding-tool-adoption-2026-developer-survey) (vendor blog; methodology not verified)

### Inferences
- The pitch "keep your price, cut your cost" matches what surveys say the most profitable AI-adopting agencies already do; Glide productizes that.
- Cost volatility of token-based tools is a stated pain; a fixed credit per delivered ticket (Glide model) directly addresses predictability, which is a selling point versus agencies wiring Claude Code/Cursor themselves.
- The hourly model penalises efficiency: every hour AI saves is lost revenue under T&M. Per-ticket resale lets the agency capture the saving.

### Gaps
- No NL-specific quantitative AI-coding adoption rate for agency developers was found (Emerce reports exist but full text not readable).
- Agency Management Institute, HubSpot and Teamleader AI/agency surveys were not retrieved in this pass.

## 5. Tools agencies pay for and price points (budget per seat/month)

### Takeaway
Agency operations software in NL/EU costs roughly EUR 10-50 per user per month (Productive $10-29, Teamleader from ~EUR 30, Simplicate from ~EUR 245/month for a 5-user minimum ≈ EUR 49/user). A 20-person agency spends in the order of EUR 600-1,000/month on its PSA/PM stack (estimate), which frames a seat-based comparison, but Glide's per-ticket price should be anchored to billable-hour value rather than to seat budgets.

### Cited Findings

## 6. Buying process: who decides, sales cycle, channels (NL)

### Takeaway
In small and mid-sized agencies the owner/founder (often with a technical lead) decides; SMB SaaS cycles are typically 1-3 months. NL channels with concentrated agency-owner attention: Dutch Digital Agencies (circles, benchmark, events), Emerce (research, Emerce 100, events), Frankwatching, Fonk/MarketingTribune, and tool ecosystems (Simplicate/Teamleader partner networks).

### Cited Findings
- Early-stage/small companies decide founder-driven with minimal process; SMB SaaS cycles ~1-3 months; broader SaaS median ~84 days — [Aexus](https://aexus.com/how-long-is-the-average-b2b-software-sales-cycle/); [Peak Sales Recruiting](https://www.peaksalesrecruiting.com/blog/saas-sales-process/) (generic SaaS sources, not agency-specific)
- DDA is the Dutch industry association for digital agencies; runs closed quarterly "circle" meetings for members, events and an annual benchmark; ~4,155 LinkedIn followers — [DDA LinkedIn](https://nl.linkedin.com/company/dutch-digital-agencies); [DDA events](https://dutchdigitalagencies.com/events/); [DDA members](https://dutchdigitalagencies.com/leden/)

- Emerce runs open calls for agencies to join its rates research (a route to reach participants) — [Emerce](https://www.emerce.nl/nieuws/doe-mee-ons-onderzoek-digital-agencytarieven)

## 7. White-label reselling by agencies and margins taken

### Takeaway
White-label reselling is established in marketing tech: agencies rebrand and resell platforms at their own price and keep the spread. GoHighLevel is the best-known example (agency pays $297-497/month, resells at ~$97-597/month per client). Reseller programs commonly offer 10-30% margins/discounts; full white-label models let the agency set price freely.

### Cited Findings
- GoHighLevel SaaS mode: agency rebrands the platform, creates client sub-accounts, sets prices and bills clients through its own Stripe; Pro plan $497/month required — [SeldonFrame](https://www.seldonframe.com/guides/gohighlevel-saas-mode-vs-flat-pricing); [Automize](https://getautomized.com/gohighlevel-pricing/)
- Agencies resell branded GoHighLevel access at $97-597/month per client (often $99-299); example: 15 clients x $297 = $4,455/month revenue on $497 cost; managed-service retainers of $497-2,997/month layered on top — [GHL guides, e.g. ghldesk](https://ghldesk.com/gohighlevel-white-label-2026/); [Grow HighLevel](https://www.grow-highlevel.com/blog/gohighlevel-white-label-pricing) (affiliate content; figures illustrative, not surveyed)
- Reseller margins typically 10-30% depending on committed volume (e.g. email/transactional platforms) — [LiveChat Partners, 50 SaaS reseller programs (2026)](https://partners.livechat.com/blog/best-reseller-programs/)
- ActiveCampaign agency program: agencies buy client accounts at a volume discount and bill clients directly under their own brand — [Vendasta white-label list](https://www.vendasta.com/blog/best-white-label-reseller-programs/)
- White-label programs let the agency apply its logo, custom domain and set its own margins; clients never see the vendor — [SuiteDash](https://suitedash.com/best-white-label-saas-reseller-programs/) (vendor)

### Inferences
- Two margin models exist: (a) discount-reseller (vendor sets list price, agency earns 10-30%), and (b) full white label (agency buys at wholesale and sets price; spreads of several x are common in GHL-style examples). Glide's model is (b); pitching it as "your cost per ticket vs. your price per ticket" mirrors GoHighLevel's SaaS-mode story that agencies already know.
- (Estimate) If an agency currently bills a small change at 2 hours x EUR 105 = EUR 210 and retains ~45-55% gross margin after labour, a Glide credit cost of EUR 20-50 per ticket plus ~15-30 minutes of review would move gross margin on that ticket to roughly 70-85% at the same client price. Proposed, not validated.

### Gaps
- No Dutch-specific evidence of agencies white-labelling software products or the margins they take was found.
- No data on how end clients react to white-label disclosure or on churn of white-label resale lines.
